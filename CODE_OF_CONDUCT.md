# Code of Conduct 🤝

## Our Pledge

We are committed to making participation in this learning repository a welcoming and respectful experience for everyone, regardless of:

- Age
- Body size
- Disability
- Ethnicity
- Gender identity and expression
- Level of experience
- Nationality
- Personal appearance
- Race
- Religion
- Sexual identity and orientation

## Our Standards

### Examples of Positive Behavior

**Be welcoming and supportive:**
- Use welcoming and inclusive language
- Respect different viewpoints and experiences
- Accept constructive feedback gracefully
- Focus on what's best for the learning community
- Show empathy toward other learners

**Be collaborative:**
- Help fellow learners when they're stuck
- Share knowledge and resources
- Celebrate others' successes
- Give credit where it's due

**Be patient:**
- Remember everyone learns at their own pace
- Don't assume knowledge - explain things clearly
- Provide context in your explanations
- Ask questions when you don't understand

### Examples of Unacceptable Behavior

- Insulting or derogatory comments
- Personal or political attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing others' private information without permission
- Trolling, intentionally disruptive behavior
- Making someone feel unwelcome or uncomfortable
- Any conduct that could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Learning Environment Guidelines

### For Contributors

**When Submitting Pull Requests:**
- Provide clear, descriptive titles
- Explain what you changed and why
- Be open to feedback and suggestions
- Don't take criticism of your code personally - it's about the code, not you
- Thank reviewers for their time

**When Reviewing Pull Requests:**
- Be kind and constructive
- Explain *why* you're suggesting changes
- Acknowledge what's done well
- Offer alternatives rather than just saying "no"
- Remember that there are humans behind the code

**Example good review comments:**
```markdown
✅ "Great work on this! I noticed a small typo on line 15..."
✅ "This is a good start. Have you considered adding an example here?"
✅ "I like this approach. Alternatively, you could also..."
✅ "Thanks for this contribution! Could you clarify what this section means?"
```

**Example poor review comments:**
```markdown
❌ "This is wrong."
❌ "Did you even read the docs?"
❌ "lol no"
❌ "Bad code"
```

### For Learners

**Asking for Help:**
- Search for existing issues/questions first
- Provide context: what you tried, what happened, what you expected
- Share relevant code snippets or error messages
- Be patient waiting for responses
- Update your question if you find the solution yourself

**Helping Others:**
- Share what you know, even if you're still learning
- It's okay to say "I don't know, but let's figure it out together"
- Point people to helpful resources
- Celebrate when they solve their problem

## Responsibilities

### Project Maintainers

Project maintainers are responsible for:
- Clarifying standards of acceptable behavior
- Taking appropriate and fair corrective action in response to unacceptable behavior
- Removing, editing, or rejecting contributions that don't align with this Code of Conduct
- Communicating reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate

### All Participants

All participants are responsible for:
- Reading and understanding this Code of Conduct
- Following these guidelines in all project spaces
- Reporting unacceptable behavior to the project maintainers
- Supporting and encouraging fellow learners

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to:
- All project spaces (repository, issues, pull requests, discussions)
- Public representation of the project
- Private communication related to project activities

## Enforcement

### Reporting Issues

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior:

1. **For minor issues:** Consider addressing it directly with the person (if comfortable)
2. **For serious issues:** Report to the project maintainers at [contact method]
3. **For GitHub-specific issues:** Use GitHub's reporting features

All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly.

### Enforcement Guidelines

Project maintainers will follow these Community Impact Guidelines:

#### 1. Correction
**Community Impact**: Inappropriate language or other unprofessional behavior.

**Consequence**: A private, written warning, with clarity about the violation and why it was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

#### 2. Warning
**Community Impact**: A violation through a single incident or series of actions.

**Consequence**: A warning with consequences for continued behavior. No interaction with the people involved for a specified period. This includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external channels.

#### 3. Temporary Ban
**Community Impact**: A serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence**: A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the community for a specified period.

#### 4. Permanent Ban
**Community Impact**: Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence**: A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the community.

## Learning from Mistakes

We recognize that:
- Everyone makes mistakes
- This is a learning environment
- What matters is how we respond when issues arise
- Growth comes from acknowledging and learning from our mistakes

If you make a mistake:
1. Acknowledge it
2. Apologize if appropriate
3. Learn from it
4. Move forward

## Best Practices for Collaboration

### Communication

**Do:**
- Use clear, simple language
- Provide examples
- Break complex ideas into smaller parts
- Ask clarifying questions
- Use "I" statements ("I think...", "In my experience...")

**Don't:**
- Use jargon without explanation
- Assume others' intentions
- Make it personal
- Use sarcasm (it doesn't translate well in text)

### Conflict Resolution

If disagreements arise:

1. **Assume good intentions** - Most misunderstandings come from miscommunication
2. **Focus on the issue, not the person** - Discuss ideas, not individuals
3. **Listen actively** - Try to understand the other perspective
4. **Find common ground** - Focus on shared goals
5. **Compromise when possible** - There are often multiple valid solutions
6. **Escalate if needed** - It's okay to ask a maintainer to help mediate
